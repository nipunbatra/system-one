2 · Where is it? Detection as many yes or no questions

A model that was never trained to point cannot give a box directly. So we cut the picture into tiles and ask the same yes or no of every tile.

Threshold for yes0.50Moving it redraws the boxes without asking the model again.

3 · One question for every tile: segmentation and more

Segmentation is a choice for every small part of the picture. Depth is a score for every part. Here each tile gets the same typed question, read in one pass.

Start with 4 × 4 tiles. A tile is cut into four and asked again only when its answer differs from a neighbour's, and the smaller cells are offered only the answers that meet at that edge. That repeats down to the smallest cell you choose, so the questions go to the edges of things and large plain areas stay cheap. A small cell is shown with a margin around it, so the model always sees at least one eighth of the picture: a very small cut-out is too little to recognise. An object that no 4 × 4 tile names is never looked at again.

The same question is asked twice: first of every tile, then by the quadtree. The two masks are shown next to each other with their passes and time.

Nothing was trainedThe model is used as downloaded. Every answer is the score of a letter token, read in one pass.
Detection, split up“Where is it?” became one yes or no per tile. Boxes are drawn around the groups of tiles that said yes, so several objects can be found.
What training buysA trained decision model such as Liquid’s d1.2 gives the four edges of a box in a single pass, without tiles.
Tiles, four at a timeThe tiles of one step do not depend on each other, so they go through the model together, four in a batch. A canvas cuts and scales each tile in a few milliseconds. With SmolVLM that makes a tile cost about a third of what it did one at a time.
